# eSIM, VPN and data on the mountain: what works in the bamboo

Most visitors start with the wrong question. They ask which VPN to buy before
they fly. The question that decides whether your maps and messages
open on Moganshan is a different one: which network is carrying your data.
A roaming SIM from home, or a China eSIM bought from a travel provider,
usually sends your traffic back through your home operator before it
reaches the internet. A Chinese SIM card does not.

Moganshan (莫干山) is a mountain in Deqing County (德清县), Huzhou (湖州),
Zhejiang (浙江), about 60 km north of Hangzhou and 200 to 240 km from
Shanghai. Moganshan Road in Shanghai, the M50 art district, is a different
place. Paying with your phone is in [paying for things on
Moganshan](/plan/paying-for-things-on-moganshan/).

## The four ways to get online

| Option | How you get it | Where your data leaves | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roaming on your home SIM | Switch it on at home | Usually your home network | Your home operator's daily price |
| A travel eSIM for China | Bought online before you fly | Depends on the provider: ask | Check the routing before you pay |
| A Chinese SIM card | Passport, in person, at an operator's own shop | Inside China | Connects inside China, under Chinese rules |
| Wifi in your room | The guesthouse or hotel | Inside China | Sign in rules vary; ask at reception |

No prices here. Roaming and travel eSIM prices come only from the sellers
themselves, and we do not quote a seller as a source.

## How a China eSIM or roaming SIM carries your data

Mobile networks give a roaming phone two ways out. In one, called home
routed, your data travels from the local mast back to your home operator and
leaves for the internet from there. In the other, local breakout, it leaves
straight from the network you are visiting. The mobile operators'
association describes both in its roaming guidelines.

> Two roaming models, Local Breakout and Home Routed, GSMA NG.113 5GS
> Roaming Guidelines, version 11.0, October 2024, tier 1.
> https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/wp-content/uploads/NG.113.v.11.0.pdf

Which one your plan uses is the thing to find out. With
home routing, the sites you reach see a connection from your own country.
That is why people roaming on foreign cards report their usual sites opening
without a VPN. In July 2025 a user of a Chinese language forum based in the
United States asked whether an American prepaid SIM roaming in China would
reach American websites. The replies said yes: roaming traffic goes home
first.

> A US prepaid SIM roaming in China reaching US sites, post by Delltv and
> replies, US Card Forum (美卡论坛), 13 and 14 July 2025, tier 3.
> https://www.uscardforum.com/t/topic/413548

Mainland users describe the mirror image. In a 2023 thread, mainland users said
their own cards route home from abroad (归属地接入, home routing), unless
they buy local data.

> Roaming routed through the home operator, local data not, thread by
> liufuyi and replies, V2EX, 25 and 26 September 2023, tier 3.
> https://www.v2ex.com/t/977077

Those are forum reports, not a guarantee. Travel eSIMs are sold by
resellers, and some break out locally. Before you pay, ask the provider in
writing where its data exits for a trip to the Chinese mainland. If the
answer is a Chinese network, plan as if you had a Chinese SIM.

![A traveller seen from behind in a station concourse, holding a phone low, a wheeled case beside them](/images/guide/esim-vpn-and-data-on-the-mountain-2.webp 'Install and test the eSIM at home, on a network you trust. A bamboo valley with one bar is a poor place to troubleshoot a new line.')

## A Chinese SIM card: a passport and a counter

The rules for a local card are strict. Bring your passport (or a
foreign permanent residence card) to a shop run by one of the operators
themselves: China Telecom (中国电信), China Mobile (中国移动), China Unicom
(中国联通) or China Broadnet (中国广电). A reseller's kiosk will not do. You
apply in person.

> SIM cards for foreigners with a passport or permanent residence card at
> the operators' own halls, packages bundling calls and data, Ministry of
> Commerce (商务部) with other departments, Guide for foreign business people
> in China, 2024 edition, Chinese embassy copy, 10 April 2024, tier 1.
> https://ss.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/dsdnflxbd/202404/t20240410_11279344.htm

Two details catch people out. Your phone has to support 5G or VoLTE (voice
over 4G), and each operator caps you at five numbers nationwide.

> Own halls only, the holder in person, a 5G or VoLTE phone, at most five
> numbers per operator nationwide, hotlines 10000, 10086 and 10010, Tianjin
> Communications Administration (天津市通信管理局), 14 April 2026, tier 1.
> https://tjca.miit.gov.cn/bsfw/bszn/art/2026/art_4cdc69ee883e409990033d44b49e0fab.html

To find a branch the operator runs itself, ring 10000 (China Telecom),
10086 (China Mobile) or 10010 (China Unicom), or use its app. Do it in
Shanghai or Hangzhou, not on the mountain.

A Chinese card is the right choice for a long stay, for a Chinese number
that apps and guesthouses can text, or when your home roaming is expensive.
It is the wrong one if you want your home apps to behave as they do at home:
a local card connects you inside China, under Chinese rules.

## The domestic eSIM is not for visitors yet

China's own operators began selling eSIMs for phones in October 2025.
Opening one still means a visit to a hall.

> eSIM phone service on sale nationwide from China Telecom, China Mobile and
> China Unicom, opened at a hall or booked in the app and finished at a
> hall, Xinhua (新华网), 14 October 2025, tier 2.
> http://www.news.cn/tech/20251014/8a25d52bf5594e36aa812f93e2deec35/c.html

When China Mobile opened bookings in September 2025, it said overseas
users were not supported for now, along with minors and anyone applying for
someone else. We found no later announcement opening it to visitors, as of
20 October 2026. For a visitor, a China eSIM still means a travel eSIM
bought abroad.

> China Mobile eSIM bookings, holder in person with an ID card, minors,
> overseas users (境外用户) and agents not supported for now, Jingchu Net
> (荆楚网), 21 September 2025, tier 2.
> https://www.ctdsb.net/c1666_202509/2542773.html

## What the VPN rule says

The rule is written down. In January 2017 the Ministry of Industry and
Information Technology (MIIT) said that without approval nobody may set up
or rent lines, VPNs included, to carry out cross border business.

> No self built or rented lines, VPNs included, for cross border operations
> without approval, MIIT notice 工信部信管函〔2017〕32号 of 17 January 2017,
> published by the Cyberspace Administration of China (中央网信办),
> 23 January 2017, tier 1.
> https://www.cac.gov.cn/2017-01/23/c_1120366809.htm

A day later the ministry added that foreign trade firms and multinationals
needing cross border links for office use could lease them from licensed
carriers. Nothing in either text speaks to a tourist's phone.

> Licensed carriers for companies' office use, MIIT clarification reported
> by China News Service (中国新闻网), 24 January 2017, tier 2.
> https://www.chinanews.com.cn/m/cj/2017/01-24/8134818.shtml

We do not advise on VPNs and do not recommend one. The practical point is
simpler: a visitor whose roaming plan or travel eSIM routes home has nothing
to decide. For them the VPN question on Moganshan doesn't arise.

Keep the roaming line on for anything that has to work.')

## Guesthouse and hotel wifi

The wifi in your room is a Chinese connection. China's Cybersecurity Law
(网络安全法) requires whoever provides network access to take your real
identity when you sign up. Since the amendment of 28 October 2025 that duty
sits in Article 26.

> Network access and mobile registration only against real identity
> information, Cybersecurity Law as amended on 28 October 2025, Article 26,
> Cyberspace Administration of China, 29 December 2025, tier 1.
> https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm

We have found no published rule on how a foreign guest signs in. Ask at
reception when you check in. The county said in 2020 that it had wifi across its
4A rated scenic areas (the national A rating runs from 1A to 5A), with the
Moganshan resort among the places it named for smart visitor services.

> Wifi across 4A scenic areas, 5G across the main town by end 2019 and the
> whole county as the 2020 aim, Zhejiang Online (浙江在线), 2 March 2020,
> tier 2.
> https://zjnews.zjol.com.cn/zjnews/huzhounews/202003/t20200302_11730781.shtml

That is a claim about the scenic areas, made in 2020. It says nothing about
the room you sleep in.

## Signal on the mountain: what is written down

Here the record runs thin. We won't fill it with a guess. Nobody has
published a signal map of the scenic area. The county's 2020 aim was 5G
everywhere in Deqing, and nobody has published whether it was met. Steep wooded valleys
weaken mobile signal anywhere, and nothing published shows Moganshan is an
exception.

One dated account helps. A walking group leader who took the Chiang Kai-shek
path loop in October 2023 noted a phone signal inside the scenic area.

> Phone signal inside the scenic area on the Chiang path loop, Youxiake
> (游侠客) community, group leader 佩琪, October 2023, tier 3.
> https://bbs.youxiake.com/y/1053344.html

That is one route on one day. Download an offline map of the mountain
before you leave the hotel. Screenshot the booking and the ticket too, and
note the way back to the transfer centre. The [Chiang Kai-shek
path](/things-to-do/chiang-kai-shek-path/) is the walk where that matters
most.

![A walker seen from behind on a narrow stone path between moso bamboo, phone in one hand, mist low among the stems](/images/guide/esim-vpn-and-data-on-the-mountain-4.webp 'Download the map at breakfast. In a bamboo valley the blue dot can stall for minutes while the path keeps going.')

## When the phone or the signal fails

A shop on a quiet lane is no place to find out your payment app has no
data, so carry some cash. It is still accepted, as [money and
payments](/plan/money-and-payments/) explains. Learn the three emergency
numbers before you go. Police is 110, fire 119 and
ambulance 120.

> Emergency numbers 110 for police, 119 for fire, 120 for ambulance,
> Ministry of Commerce (商务部) with other departments, Guide for foreign
> business people in China, 10 April 2024, tier 1.
> https://ss.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/dsdnflxbd/202404/t20240410_11279344.htm

Other questions are answered on the [plan your trip FAQ](/plan/faq/) and
under [plan your trip](/plan/).
