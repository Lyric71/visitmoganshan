# Hot springs at Moganshan

We cannot confirm a hot spring open to the public at Moganshan today. Moganshan (莫干山) stands in Deqing County, north of Hangzhou by about 60 km, in the Huzhou prefecture of Zhejiang Province. Shanghai is 200 to 240 km away. One sentence in one hotel press release is the whole of the evidence. It is worth reading closely, because it is all there is.

## The one thing that is verified

Four Seasons announced a resort here on 12 September 2025. It runs to 90 keys on a site of 23 hectares, and opens in 2030. The announcement calls it "Moganshan's first international luxury resort with direct access to natural hot springs".

> Four Seasons Moganshan announcement, 12 September 2025, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

That phrase is the strongest evidence in our research that natural hot springs exist on or near this mountain. It is also a hotel's marketing claim rather than a geological survey, and our research turned up nothing behind it: no named source for the water, no temperature. Weigh it accordingly.

Then weigh the date. Even if the water is exactly as described, the resort holding that "direct access" does not open for another four years. Our page on [that announcement](/where-to-stay/hotels/four-seasons-moganshan/) carries the project detail.

The last row decides whether the claim means much, because "direct access" is a flexible phrase. It could describe a spring under the property, or a shuttle to a bathhouse in the next valley. The announcement does not say, and neither will we.

## Hot spring hotels on the mountain

No property here is on record as offering hot spring bathing. Not the resorts, not the guesthouses. If you are picturing an outdoor pool steaming in the bamboo, nobody has shown us one. That is not the same as knowing it is not there.

The search results will not settle it either, because these words get used loosely.

| The label | What it can mean | What we found here |
|---|---|---|
| Hot spring | Water heated underground, or an ordinary heated pool with a better name | No figures published anywhere we looked |
| Onsen | Japanese for a natural hot spring bath, borrowed in English listings for any warm water | No onsen, and no bathhouse either |
| Hot spring hotel | A property with bathing on site, or one selling access to water somewhere else | None found here |

So a label settles nothing, which sends most readers to the next question: where else, then.

## Hot springs near Shanghai

We researched this mountain between 1 and 5 August 2026, and not the region around it. Resorts elsewhere in the Yangtze delta sit outside what we checked, so we cannot point you to one, and we will not name places we have never looked at.

Here is a test that works anywhere. A destination that really has a hot spring will name the source and publish a water temperature. That is the detail Moganshan does not have. If soaking in natural mineral water is the reason for the trip, go where the numbers are printed. Then come here for what this mountain actually has.

## Cool air, and water in the landscape

This is not what you came for, but it is what is here.

Cool air, first. The mountain's whole reputation runs the other way from a hot spring: people have come up here since the 1890s to get cooler, not warmer. July and August here average 24.1 °C, a gap of 6 to 7 °C against Shanghai and Hangzhou. American missionaries built a summer town on this ridge for exactly that reason. [Moganshan weather](/moganshan/weather/) has the month-by-month figures.

## If your trip depends on the water

Say you are coming anyway, and the water is still the point. Confirm it with a property before you book, not after. [Where to stay](/where-to-stay/) lists what exists here, by village and price band. Email rather than phone, because you want the answer in writing, and keep the questions narrow:

- Is your water naturally hot from the ground, or heated?
- Is the bath on your site, and if not, how far away is it?
- Is it open on our dates, and is it included in your rate?

A clear yes, with a distance and a price, is an answer. Anything softer is a maybe, so book on the assumption that there is no hot spring. That is what the record supports.
