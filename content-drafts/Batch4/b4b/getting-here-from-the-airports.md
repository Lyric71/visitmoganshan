# Getting to Moganshan from Shanghai and Hangzhou airports

Three airports can put you on this mountain. Between the quickest of them and the slowest sits two to three hours, depending on how each end of your trip goes. That gap is set by geography rather than by how well you plan. Better to know it while the flight is still a browser tab.

The destination does not move. Moganshan stands in Deqing County, part of Huzhou prefecture in Zhejiang Province, with Hangzhou about 60 km south of it and Shanghai 200 to 240 km back. Every route in ends the same way, at a high-speed station called Deqing followed by about half an hour of road. All that changes is how much of your day disappears before you reach that station.

The short answer, one line each.

- **Shanghai Hongqiao (SHA).** The easy one. The rail station is at the airport, so you walk across and ride 63 to 80 minutes.
- **Shanghai Pudong (PVG).** The hard one. You cross Shanghai before the journey starts, which is where the four hours come from.
- **Hangzhou Xiaoshan (HGH).** The closest, and more than two hours ahead of Pudong by car.

One booking warning that costs people a day. Book the train to **Deqing (德清)**. A station called Moganshan exists on a different line and does not serve the mountain. Get that wrong and you land at Wukang, 20 km off, and finish by road.

## The three airports compared

Read every figure below as a whole journey: the walk, the wait, the train, the road transfer and the last stretch to the door. A Friday in July runs longer, and so does any national holiday. The times are our estimates. Everything behind them, the fares and the service counts, is measured.

| Airport | Road distance | Door to door (estimate) | Ground cost per person, one way | Same-day arrival sensible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hongqiao (SHA)** | About 200 to 240 km | 2h45 to 3h30 | RMB 61 to 110 rail, plus RMB 10 shuttle or about RMB 100 taxi | Yes, if you land before about midday |
| **Pudong (PVG)** | 200 to 240 km plus the cross-city leg | 4h00 to 4h45 | The same, plus that cross-city leg | Only on a morning arrival, and it still makes a long day |
| **Xiaoshan (HGH)** | More than the 60 to 63 km from central Hangzhou | 1h45 to 2h15 by car, 2h30 to 3h00 by rail | RMB 16 to 67 rail, plus the last mile and the run into Hangzhou East | Yes, with the widest margin of the three |

The last column is the one that decides your evening, and the flight schedule has little to do with it. Two closing times settle it: the last shuttle from Deqing leaves at 16:00, and the scenic-area transfer centers shut at 18:00. Those two are the only hard numbers on this page. Everything else moves.

The 18:00 close is also where the midday cutoff in the Hongqiao row comes from, and you should check it. Count back from 18:00 through a 3h30 door-to-door and an hour for immigration and baggage, and the last safe landing is 13:30. Midday is that with an hour of slack.

Each airport in turn now, easiest first.

## Hongqiao: walk to the train

The airport and Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station sit on the same site. You clear immigration and then walk to your train, without leaving the complex. No taxi, no crossing the city between the plane and the platform. That is why people call this trip easy.

From there, 13 direct services a day run to Deqing. The ride takes 63 to 80 minutes and a second class seat costs roughly RMB 61 to 110, so the train you pick moves the price more than you might expect.

| Train | Departs Hongqiao | Arrives Deqing | Journey |
|---|---|---|---|
| D377 | 08:11 | 09:19 | 68 min |
| G687 | 12:12 | 13:21 | 69 min |
| G1419 | 17:39 | 18:42 | 63 min |

Those three are only a sample of the day, and the last one carries a warning. The 17:39 reaches Deqing at 18:42, after the shuttle has finished and the transfer centers have shut. Only the first two land you inside the working day.

Two things slow this down more than the map suggests. The station is huge and security queues build fast. And a Chinese rail ticket is tied to the document that bought it, so book on the passport you will carry.

At Deqing you still have about 30 minutes of road. The Y1, a tourist shuttle bus, runs from the coach center beside the station for RMB 10 in 30 to 40 minutes. A taxi or van covers the same ground in about 30 minutes for about RMB 100. Booking detail is on our [Shanghai to Moganshan by train](/getting-here/from-shanghai/by-train/) page, and the arrival end on the [Deqing station](/getting-here/deqing-station/) page.

## Pudong: the airport that costs you a day

Pudong sits on the far east edge of Shanghai. Moganshan is southwest of the city. So before the 63 to 80 minute train even starts, you have to cross Shanghai to Hongqiao.

That crossing runs 50 to 70 minutes by car. Maglev then metro is faster on paper and slower in practice, once you add two suitcases and two changes.

## Xiaoshan: closest, and mostly overlooked

Two airports down, both in Shanghai. Xiaoshan sits beside the other city and is the nearest of the three to Moganshan. It hardly appears in English-language advice about the mountain. We have not established why. If your flights are not booked yet, this is the only section on the page that can still change something.

The arithmetic is the argument. By car Xiaoshan is 2 hours 15 to 2 hours 30 quicker than Pudong. By rail it is still an hour and a half ahead.

| Route from Xiaoshan | The chain | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Rail | Into Hangzhou East, high-speed train to Deqing, then shuttle or taxi | 2h30 to 3h00 |
| Private car | Straight through to a transfer center | 1h45 to 2h15 |

## If you land late

This is the section that decides whether tonight works. Two things shut down while you are still in the air, and neither is on your itinerary. Work it out now, on a laptop, not at 19:00 in an arrivals hall with a dying phone.

**The Y1 shuttle's last departure from Deqing is 16:00.** Seven services run in a day, and that is the whole timetable: 07:30, 08:40, 09:40, 10:40, 13:00, 15:00 and 16:00. Nothing runs between 10:40 and 13:00 either.

**The scenic-area transfer centers close at 18:00.** Private vehicles cannot drive inside the core scenic area. Everyone changes at Yucun, Fatou or Houwu, and all three operate 08:00 to 18:00. After that, a bed inside the boundary cannot be reached by road at all.

Whether that rule applies to you is not something a map tells you. Many guesthouse villages sit outside the boundary, where a taxi reaches the door at any hour. Ask the property which side it is on.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Bed is outside the scenic area | Taxi from Deqing. About RMB 100, about 30 minutes |
| Bed is inside the scenic area | Message the property before you board the train, not after you land. Ask where they can meet you |
| No reply, and it is dark | Stay in Wukang, the Deqing county seat about 20 km off, and go up in the morning |

There is a fourth option. It is the best one and almost nobody books it: put night one in Wukang or Shanghai on purpose, and the problem never arrives.

Eat before you go up, wherever you sleep. Chinese reporting notes the Moganshan villages have no evening economy, meaning no night market and very little late-night food. Reaching a village hungry at 21:00 happens often enough to plan around.

![A village lane at Moganshan after dark, lit only by a guesthouse doorway](/images/guide/from-the-airports-4.webp 'Arriving after dark, this is the whole of the evening. The kitchens have closed and the transfer centers closed three hours earlier.')

## Cars and private transfers

A car has come up in every section above. It removes the changes. It does not remove the boundary. Your driver stops at a transfer center like everyone else, and after 18:00 the system has shut.

It earns its cost in four situations:

- A family with luggage, where the shuttle means four cases on a bus
- A group of four, where the fare splits and stops looking expensive
- Any arrival outside shuttle hours
- A guesthouse in an outlying village, off the shuttle route

Book through the property rather than at the curb, and ask which transfer center serves them. They field that question every week.
