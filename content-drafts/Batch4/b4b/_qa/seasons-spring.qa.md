## Iteration 2 — 10 weaknesses

1. **Length.** 1,805 body words against a 1,300 target, about 39 percent over. The page is not denser than the target, it is just longer, so the overrun is fat rather than substance.
2. **Corpus duplication in the "what to wear" section.** The Y1 shuttle timetable, the 08:00 to 18:00 transfer window and the ticket-price conflict are already carried on the weather, summer, autumn and winter pages. Reproducing all three here adds nothing a spring reader needs and makes the section read as boilerplate.
3. **The tea section is a compressed copy of /moganshan/tea/.** Mingqian and yuqian, the grades, the warning about prepaid picking experiences: all of it lives on the tea page in more detail. This page should carry only the part that is about spring, and hand the rest over.
4. **The spring-against-autumn table has an invented row and an unlabeled opinion column.** "Color: new growth everywhere" is not in the fact base and is doing decorative work. The "Our call" column reads as a verdict when it is a judgment, and nothing says so.
5. **A fabricated price point.** "Rooms that cost RMB 400 in March clear RMB 1,000" invents RMB 400. The fact base gives a band of RMB 300 to 500, never a single figure. This breaks the house rule against inventing a price.
6. **Unlabeled inference presented as reportage.** "The week before and the week after are ordinary spring" is our reasoning about how a holiday spike decays. No source in the fact base says it.
7. **The Golden Week advice is not actionable.** It says to check the dates without saying why they move, so a reader who does not know that Chinese public holidays are announced annually with adjusted working weekends is left with a warning and no method.
8. **The opening pre-announces its own structure.** "So the page is built from what spring can be proved on: a harvest, a tea pick, a room rate and one very bad week" is signposting, a standard machine tell, and it spends 25 words describing the page instead of being it.
9. **The temperature section makes one point four times.** Reasoning not measuring, then the subtraction line, then no instrument, then the valley forecast, then no rainfall record. Two of those five are the argument and three are restatement.
10. **The structure sags in the middle.** Prose front, three tables stacked across the last four sections. A reader hits an unbroken run of tables at exactly the point the page should be arguing.
