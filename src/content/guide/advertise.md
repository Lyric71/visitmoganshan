# Advertise with us

This site is read by people deciding whether to come to this mountain, where to sleep when they do, and what to do with the two days they have. If you run something those people pay for, you can reach them here.

Visit Moganshan is an English language guide to Moganshan, published from Hong Kong by BeyondBorder Group Ltd. It is written for travellers coming from Shanghai and Hangzhou, and for the smaller number who fly into China with the mountain already on the list. Nearly every visitor to this site is in the planning window: still choosing, not yet booked.

## Who this is for

Anything a visitor spends money on is in scope. In practice that means:

* Hotels and resorts, from a twelve room villa conversion to a full property
* Guesthouses and minsu, including the ones that never appear in English anywhere else
* Restaurants, cafés, tea houses and bars
* Attractions, museums, gardens and paid viewpoints
* Spas, pools, climbing walls, bamboo rafting, archery, anything sold by the session
* Walking guides, cycling operators, drivers and airport transfer services
* Tour operators, DMCs and inbound agencies building product around the mountain
* Shops selling something a visitor carries home, bamboo, tea, silk

![A row of near identical small guesthouses with tiled roofs along a village road](/images/guide/advertise-2.webp 'About a thousand guesthouses sit within a short drive of the gate, and almost none of them are findable in English.')

You do not need to be inside the scenic area. Deqing, Wuzhen, Anji, Hangzhou and the Shanghai departure end of the trip are all part of the same journey, and a reader planning a Moganshan weekend books more than a bed on it.

## What the audience is, and what we will not claim

Two honest sentences before the sales part.

This site is new. Visit Moganshan published its first pages in 2026, and we do not yet have twelve months of traffic behind us. We will not quote you a monthly visitor number in a public page and we will not quote you one we cannot show you in an analytics screen. Ask, and you get the figures as they stand on the day you ask, including the ones that are small.

What we can describe with confidence is the shape of the audience rather than its size. The language is English. The intent is planning: the pages that get read are the ones about getting here, where to stay, what the weather does in each month, and whether the trip is worth making at all. That is a reader who has not yet spent anything and is about to.

## The plans

Three of them, deliberately few, because a page cluttered with paid boxes stops being useful and a reader who stops trusting a page is worth nothing to anybody advertising on it.

| Plan | What it is | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Listing | An expanded profile for your property or business, with your own photographs, your own contact details and a direct booking link | 12 months |
| Placement | A labelled slot on the guide pages a reader for your business actually lands on | 3 or 12 months |
| Feature | One sponsored article about your property or service, written to the same standard as the rest of the site and labelled as paid | Permanent, kept current |

**Listing** is the entry point, and it suits a property that already exists on the site as one of several hundred and wants its own page to be better than the data feed can make it. You supply photographs you have the right to publish, a description of what you actually offer, opening hours, rates and a way to be contacted. We write the page in the site's voice, publish it, and keep it current when you tell us something moved.

**Placement** puts you on the pages that matter for you rather than on all of them. A hotel belongs on the accommodation pages and on the itinerary pages for the seasons it fills. A transfer service belongs on the getting here pages, which are the most read on the site, because the thirty kilometres between Deqing station and the mountain is the part of this trip everyone gets wrong. We will tell you which pages we think fit you, and we will tell you when we think none of them do.

**Feature** is a full article about you, researched, illustrated and marked as sponsored at the top of the page and in the page source. It reads like the rest of the site because it is written like the rest of the site. It carries the same last checked date and the same sourcing, and it stays up.

![Four walkers with day packs climbing a village lane between old houses toward bamboo hills](/images/guide/advertise-3.webp 'The reader this site reaches is still deciding. By the time they are on the lane, the booking is already made.')

Bundles exist and are cheaper than the sum of the parts. A property that takes a listing and a placement on the two pages that suit it is the arrangement we would suggest to most people who write in.

## What advertising does not buy

This part is not decoration and it is not negotiable.

It does not buy an editorial mention. Where a page names properties, it names them on the same terms whether they pay or not, and paying does not add a name to that list.

It does not buy a position. There is no ranking on this site that money moves.

It does not buy the removal of a caveat. Every property page carries one honest trade off a reader can act on, and it is written before any commercial conversation, not after.

It does not buy a rating. We do not rate or score properties, for advertisers or for anyone else, because we have not stayed in them.

It does not buy silence. If a reader sends us a correction about your business and it holds up, we make it, and being an advertiser does not change that.

If those terms are a problem, we are the wrong site and it is better for both of us to find that out in the first email.

## Everything paid is labelled

Sponsored content says sponsored, in the page, where a reader sees it before they read the copy rather than after. Commercial links are marked `rel="sponsored"` in the source, which is how you tell a search engine a link is paid. That costs us search value and we do it anyway. [How this site is funded](/plan/disclosure/) sets out the whole policy, including the affiliate commissions we already earn on some booking links.

![A thick stack of printed page proofs with a pen laid on top, beside a laptop on a dark desk](/images/guide/advertise-4.webp 'What we need from you is the boring half: rates, hours, the licence on your photographs, and someone who answers email.')

## What we need from you

Photographs you own or have written permission to publish, at a decent size. The correct name of the business in English and in Chinese. Your rates or rate bands, and the season they apply to. Opening hours, and the months you close. An address a driver can find. A phone number or an email that a real person reads. If you take bookings through a platform, the link.

That is most of the work, and the campaigns that go badly are the ones where it arrives in pieces over six weeks.

## Rates

There is no published rate card yet. Inventory on this site is small and every arrangement so far has been priced against what it actually delivers, so we would rather quote you than publish a number that fits nobody.

Tell us what you run, which of the three plans looks right, and roughly what you were thinking of spending. You get a written quote, the pages we propose putting you on, and what we can and cannot promise about the traffic they carry.
