# Can a guesthouse refuse your passport? Foreign guest registration in rural Zhejiang

Not on the ground it usually gives. No Chinese law says a guesthouse needs a
special licence to take a foreigner on a passport, whatever a China homestay
listing claims. On 24 May 2024 three national bodies said in writing that
hotels may not turn foreigners away for want of one. What the law does ask is
registration: the house records your passport and reports it to the police.

That is the rule. The listings say otherwise. On 13 October 2026 we read the
guest policy on every one of the 809 Moganshan properties in our Trip.com
list. Of the 805 still online, 308 said they take mainland Chinese documents
only. And 228 of those gave the reason as "local regulations".

> Guests Accepted lines on 805 live Moganshan listings, BeyondBorder Group
> Ltd primary research, Trip.com listing pages read 13 October 2026.

| Question | Short answer | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Can a house refuse you because it has no foreign guest licence? | No. Three national bodies said so on 24 May 2024 | A joint reply, tier 2 |
| Must it register your passport? | Yes, and report it to the police within 24 hours | National hotel measures, tier 1 |
| Do Moganshan listings still say Chinese ID only? | 308 of 805 did on 13 October 2026, most citing "local regulations" | Our own reading of Trip.com |
| A private villa or a friend's house? | You or the host register within 24 hours, online in Zhejiang since 20 March 2026 | National Immigration Administration, tier 1 |
| The fine for not registering | A warning, and up to RMB 2,000 | Exit and Entry Law, tier 1 |

Moganshan (莫干山) is a mountain in Deqing County (德清县), Huzhou (湖州), Zhejiang
(浙江), about 60 km north of Hangzhou and 200 to 240 km from Shanghai. Moganshan
Road in Shanghai, the M50 art district, is a different place. Most beds here
are in minsu (民宿), small owner run guesthouses, and [what a minsu
is](/where-to-stay/minsu-explained/) explains the form.

This piece is desk written. The plan was to ring 20 to 30 guesthouses and ask.
Nobody here rang anyone. What follows is the written record, read in Chinese
first, and what the listings themselves say.

## China homestay rules for foreigners: a passport, not a licence

The Exit and Entry Administration Law (出境入境管理法) splits foreign guests in two.
In a hotel (旅馆), the hotel registers you and reports your details to the local
police. Anywhere else, you or your host register with the police within 24
hours of moving in.

> Hotel registration and the 24 hour rule elsewhere, Exit and Entry
> Administration Law, articles 39 and 76, National Immigration
> Administration (国家移民管理局), page dated 21 June 2019, tier 1.
> https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html

The national hotel measures fill in the detail. A hotel needs a special trade
licence (特种行业许可证) from the police before it opens. It must register every
guest and check the identity document. For a guest from outside the mainland
it sends the registration form to the police within 24 hours.

> Licence, registration and the 24 hour report, Hotel Industry Public
> Security Measures (旅馆业治安管理办法), articles 4 and 6, revised 29 March
> 2022, Ministry of Commerce law database (商务部 全球法规网), tier 1.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=94684

Guesthouses in Deqing sit under the same rules. The county's minsu measures of
January 2014 resolved the fire safety and special trade licence problems that
small houses had faced.

> Deqing's 2014 minsu measures and the special trade licence, China Rural
> Net (中国农村网), 19 March 2019, tier 2.
> http://journal.crnews.net/zgcz/2018n/d11q/927017_20190319030107.html

So a licensed minsu on Moganshan is, for this purpose, a hotel. It registers
you, and you don't go near a police station.

Neither text says a hotel needs a second licence for foreigners. That idea is
older than the law. Jiemian (界面新闻) traced it to a September 1988 star rating
rule that licensed "tourism (foreign related)" hotels. Beijing police stopped
steering foreigners to designated hotels from 1 October 2003. Tianjin dropped
its approval step a month later, and Henan in 2005.

> The 1988 rule and the 2003 change in Beijing, Jiemian (界面新闻),
> 28 May 2024, tier 2.
> https://www.jiemian.com/article/11220490.html

## The 24 May 2024 reply, and what it is not

Foreigners from Nigeria, the UK and Pakistan wrote to the State Council's
website (中国政府网). Hotels had refused them, saying they were "not qualified to
receive foreigners" or "did not know how to enter the information into the
system". It was worse in small cities and budget chains, they said.

On 24 May 2024 the Ministry of Public Security, the Ministry of Commerce and
the National Immigration Administration answered. They were studying measures,
they wrote, and "require the hotel trade not to refuse foreign guests on the
ground of having no foreign related qualification" (要求旅馆业不得以无涉外资质为由拒绝接待境外人员).
The commerce ministry had the China Hotel Association issue a call to make
foreign guests welcome, and the police were told to make registration simpler.

> The joint reply of 24 May 2024, China Tourism News (中国旅游新闻网),
> relaying the State Council website, 24 May 2024, tier 2.
> https://www.ctnews.com.cn/chanye/content/2024-05/24/content_160606.html

Be clear about what that is. A published reply to public messages, with no
document number. It removes one excuse, the missing licence, and names no
penalty for a house that refuses anyway.

## Why the refusals did not stop

Four days later a Southern Metropolis Daily (南方都市报) reporter tested it in
Beijing. On the afternoon of 28 May a chain hotel said it had no foreign guest
qualification, did not take foreigners and had not heard of the reply. The
reporter then rang two police stations. One said a foreigner had to register
there first. The other suggested trying a different hotel.

> Refusals four days after the reply, Southern Metropolis Daily
> (南方都市报), 28 May 2024, tier 2.
> https://m.mp.oeeee.com/a/BAAFRD000020240529958753.html

The reasons were mundane. Hotels told Jiemian that registering a foreigner was
fiddly and felt risky. A hotel worker in Shenzhen said one wrong entry in the
police system meant a fine, "or at worst suspension".

> Why hotels avoid foreign guests, Jiemian (界面新闻), 28 May 2024, tier 2.
> https://www.jiemian.com/article/11220490.html

The law backs that fear. A hotel that fails to report a foreign guest's
details gets a warning, and a fine of RMB 1,000 to 5,000 if the case is
serious. The 2024 reply carries no matching cost for saying no. For a family
house that rarely sees a foreign booking, the arithmetic points one way.

> Penalties for a hotel that does not register or report a foreign guest,
> Exit and Entry Administration Law, article 76, National Immigration
> Administration (国家移民管理局), page dated 21 June 2019, tier 1.
> https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html

## What Moganshan's listings say about minsu foreign guests

That was Beijing in 2024. On the mountain, two years on, the listings
themselves give the answer.

Every Moganshan property page we read on Trip.com carries a line headed Guests
Accepted, among its policies. The English site shows it to foreign users, so a
"mainland only" line is a refusal printed in advance. Jiemian found the same
line, 可接待人群, on Chinese booking sites in 2024. It is the closest thing to a
written answer a house gives before you book.

We read that line on all 809 properties in our Moganshan list between 22:13
and 22:50 on 13 October 2026, from each property's own page. Nothing was
booked and nobody was asked. Four pages had gone. Here is what the other 805
said.

| Guests Accepted line on the listing | Listings | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Guests from all countries and regions are welcome | 497 | 62 percent |
| Mainland Chinese ID cards only, "to comply with local regulations" | 228 | 28 percent |
| Mainland issued travel permit, residence permit or ID card only | 80 | 10 percent |

> Guests Accepted lines on 805 live Moganshan listings, BeyondBorder Group
> Ltd primary research, Trip.com listing pages read 13 October 2026.

Read the second row twice. The 228 houses that cite "local regulations" are
citing a rule the three national bodies say does not exist. And the 80 permit
listings, read as written, may take a foreigner who lives in China on a
mainland residence permit, or a Hong Kong, Macau or Taiwan resident on a
mainland issued travel permit. A visitor on a passport alone is outside both
groups.

The pattern shifts with the kind of property.

| Listings, by name or address | Live listings | Mainland documents only |
|---|---|---|
| "Villa" in the name | 116 | 57 |
| "Hotel" or "resort" in the name | 155 | 46 |
| "Homestay", "guesthouse" or "inn" in the name | 270 | 104 |
| Address in Houwu | 103 | 32 |
| Address in Xiantan | 95 | 35 |
| Address in Laoling | 58 | 13 |

> Breakdown of the same 805 listings by name and by listed address,
> BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 13 October 2026.

Listings named as villas say no most often, about half of them. Places that
call themselves hotels are the likeliest to say yes, seven in ten. The village
pages for [Houwu](/moganshan/villages/houwu/) and
[Xiantan](/moganshan/villages/xiantan/) cover where each sits. About a quarter
of listings name no village in the address, so we have not tallied the rest by
place.

One caution runs both ways. A listing line is what a property told the
platform. It is not a test at the door. A "welcome" house can still balk, and
a "mainland only" house might say yes if asked.

## Checking in with a passport in Huzhou

The paperwork is getting lighter here. In March 2025 Huzhou police said about
120 hotels and guesthouses across the city had a reader called e境通 (e
Jingtong). It reads passports from more than 150 countries with 99.5 percent
accuracy. Check in takes under a minute, against six or seven by hand.

> Passport readers at about 120 hotels and guesthouses in Huzhou, Zhejiang
> Daily (浙江日报) on Hangzhou Net (杭州网), 25 March 2025, tier 2.
> https://news.hangzhou.com.cn/zjnews/content/2025-03/25/content_8959193.htm

Deqing's own police opened Huzhou's first county level visa point for
foreigners, and immigration service stations in five parts of the county where
foreigners gather. They sit where foreign residents live. The 2025 answer
quoted below names foreigner service stations among the places a registration
can be made in person.

> Deqing visa point and five immigration service stations, Huzhou Public
> Security Bureau (湖州市公安局) official account, on Tencent News,
> 24 September 2024, tier 1.
> https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20240924A03P1900

Bring the passport itself, not a photograph of it. The measures say the house
checks the identity document, and the details it records come off your entry
stamp as well as the photo page. If you hold two passports, check in on the
one you entered China with.

![A traveller seen from behind walking up a wet village lane between guesthouses, a rucksack on one shoulder and autumn bamboo above](/images/guide/can-a-guesthouse-refuse-your-passport-foreign-3.webp 'Huzhou police say the same reader also takes Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan travel permits and permanent residence cards.')

## A rented villa or a friend's flat: you register

Not every bed on the mountain is in a licensed house. If you rent a home from
its owner, or stay with friends, the second half of article 39 applies. You or
your host register with the police within 24 hours.

Since 20 March 2026 that can be done online in seven provinces, Zhejiang among
them. The channels are the National Immigration Administration's website, its
移民局12367 app (NIA 12367), the app's WeChat mini program and an Alipay mini
program. An online registration counts the same as one made at a police
station, and the station remains an option.

> Online registration outside hotels in seven provinces from 20 March 2026,
> National Immigration Administration (国家移民管理局), announcement 2026
> No. 1, 20 March 2026, tier 1.
> https://www.nia.gov.cn/n897453/c1771531/content.html

One catch. The first registration at an address is made with the host's help,
because the host knows the address details. Help is on the 12367 hotline, or
at the local police station. Skip it and article 76 applies: a warning, and a
fine of up to RMB 2,000.

> Host's help first time, the hotline and the penalty, National Immigration
> Administration (国家移民管理局), policy interpretation, 20 March 2026,
> tier 1.
> https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741577/c1771556/content.html

Eight months earlier it could not be done here. In July 2025 a Dutch user
asked to register online "whether in a minsu or a friend's home". The answer
then named a police station, a community police office or a foreigner service
station. Beijing, Shanghai, Hainan and Chongqing were among the places already
taking registrations online. Zhejiang was not named.

> Registration outside hotels in July 2025, Xinhua (新华网), relaying the
> State Council website, 16 July 2025, tier 2.
> https://www.news.cn/government/20250716/b8ee6b49c5814c17b007815fd3f6b5d9/c.html

Which route applies depends on the property, not on you. A licensed house
registers you itself, while an owner letting a villa privately leaves it to
the two of you. Ask which it is. [Villas
explained](/where-to-stay/villas-explained/) sets out how those rentals work.

![A rented hillside house terrace on a grey autumn morning, two suitcases by the glass door and a set of keys on a wet table](/images/guide/can-a-guesthouse-refuse-your-passport-foreign-4.webp 'The 24 hours run from when you move in, not from when you land in China.')

## The message to send before you pay

Read the Guests Accepted line first. If it says mainland documents only, book
elsewhere. If it says all countries, write anyway, before any money moves.
Something like this, in English and Chinese:

"I will check in on a foreign passport. Can you register foreign guests?"
(我持外国护照入住，请问贵店可以办理外国人住宿登记吗？)

A house used to foreign guests answers in a line. One that goes quiet has
answered too. Keep the reply on your phone for the desk.

If a house says it lacks the licence, the 2024 reply is the answer, and you
can show the house the Chinese sentence quoted in the reply section, then find
another house. The reply names no penalty for a refusal, and the law penalises
a failure to register, not a refusal to take you.

Questions on registration itself go to the 12367 hotline. Getting into the
country is a separate question, covered in [China visa free
entry](/plan/china-visa-free-entry/), and the [planning FAQ](/plan/faq/) takes
the shorter ones.

Trip.com's list for the [resort zone](/go/stays-all/) links each property's
own page. The Guests Accepted line sits under its policies, near the check in
times.
