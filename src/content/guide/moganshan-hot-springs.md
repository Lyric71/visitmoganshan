# Hot springs at Moganshan

There is no hot spring open to the public at Moganshan today. Moganshan (莫干山) stands in Deqing County, north of Hangzhou by about 60 km, in the Huzhou prefecture of Zhejiang Province. Shanghai is 200 to 240 km away. One sentence in one hotel press release is the whole of the evidence that hot water exists here at all. It is worth reading closely, because it is all there is.

## The one line the claim rests on

Four Seasons announced a resort here on 12 September 2025. It runs to 90 keys on a site of 23 hectares, and opens in 2030. The announcement calls it "Moganshan's first international luxury resort with direct access to natural hot springs".

![Steam rising from a still dark outdoor pool at dawn](/images/guide/moganshan-hot-springs-2.webp 'One line in one press release is the entire published evidence. That is not the same as a hot spring you can visit.')

> Four Seasons Moganshan announcement, 12 September 2025, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

That phrase is the strongest claim anybody has made that natural hot springs exist on or near this mountain. It is also a hotel's marketing line rather than a geological survey, with no named source for the water and no temperature behind it. Weigh it accordingly.

Then weigh the date. Even if the water is exactly as described, the resort holding that "direct access" does not open for another four years. Our page on [that announcement](/where-to-stay/hotels/four-seasons-moganshan/) carries the project detail.

## What the announcement does not say

Everything a visitor would need to know sits outside that sentence. No source is named for the water, no temperature is given, no mineral analysis has been published, and nothing establishes whether "direct access" means a spring under the property or a shuttle to a bathhouse in the next valley.

Those are the details a genuine hot spring destination publishes as a matter of course, because they are the selling point. Their absence here is the answer.

## Hot spring hotels on the mountain

No property here offers hot spring bathing. Not the resorts, not the guesthouses. If you are picturing an outdoor pool steaming in the bamboo, that picture belongs to a different mountain.

The search results will not settle it either, because these words get used loosely.

| The label | What it can mean | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| Hot spring | Water heated underground, or an ordinary heated pool with a better name | No published figures for either |
| Onsen | Japanese for a natural hot spring bath, borrowed in English listings for any warm water | No onsen, and no bathhouse either |
| Hot spring hotel | A property with bathing on site, or one selling access to water somewhere else | Not a category that exists on this mountain |

So a label settles nothing, which sends most readers to the next question: where else, then.

## Hot springs near Shanghai

This site covers Moganshan rather than the wider Yangtze delta, so it is not the place to shop for a spa resort elsewhere in the region.

Here is a test that works anywhere. A destination that really has a hot spring will name the source and publish a water temperature. That is the detail Moganshan does not have. If soaking in natural mineral water is the reason for the trip, go where the numbers are printed. Then come here for what this mountain actually has.

## Cool air, and water in the landscape

This is not what you came for, but it is what is here.

![A clear mountain stream running over dark rock through bamboo](/images/guide/moganshan-hot-springs-3.webp 'The water on this mountain is cold, fast and everywhere. It is the thing the landscape is actually built on.')

Cool air, first. The mountain's whole reputation runs the other way from a hot spring: people have come up here since the 1890s to get cooler, not warmer. July and August here average 24.1 °C, a gap of 6 to 7 °C against Shanghai and Hangzhou. American missionaries built a summer town on this ridge for exactly that reason. [Moganshan weather](/moganshan/weather/) has the month-by-month figures.

Then water, though not the kind you get into.

| Where | What it is |
|---|---|
| Sword Pond (Jianchi) | The named sight inside the scenic area, tied to the swordsmith legend the mountain is named for |
| Laoling | A reservoir on the south side, with kayaking and cycling |
| Dazaowu | The most remote village, with rice paddies and the Dadouwu reservoir |

> Named sights, villages and reservoirs, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

Swimming in the reservoirs is not a thing anybody organises here. Kayaking at Laoling is, arranged through the guesthouses down there. [Things to do](/things-to-do/) covers the rest.

## If your trip depends on the water

Say you are coming anyway, and the water is still the point. Confirm it with a property before you book, not after. [Where to stay](/where-to-stay/) lists what exists here, by village and price band. Email rather than phone, because you want the answer in writing, and keep the questions narrow:

![A still indoor hotel pool with a glass wall looking onto bamboo](/images/guide/moganshan-hot-springs-4.webp 'Several hotels have heated pools. None of that water comes out of the ground hot.')

- Is your water naturally hot from the ground, or heated?
- Is the bath on your site, and if not, how far away is it?
- Is it open on our dates, and is it included in your rate?

A clear yes, with a distance and a price, is an answer. Anything softer is a maybe, so book on the assumption that there is no hot spring.
