# Corporate retreats in Moganshan

Two things make Moganshan a serious candidate for a corporate retreat. The trip costs half a day, not a whole one, and midweek the mountain has beds standing empty.

Assume a group of twenty to fifty, and assume the hard part is moving that many adults out of the city and back inside a work week. The destination is a mountain in Zhejiang Province, in Deqing County of Huzhou prefecture, 200 to 240 km out of Shanghai and 60 km from Hangzhou. (Moganshan Road, an art district in Shanghai's Putuo District, sits 200 km from here and is not the same place.)

## The travel math

| From | Route | Door to door |
|---|---|---|
| Central Shanghai | Private car or hired coach | 2h30 to 3h15 |
| Shanghai Hongqiao | High-speed rail to Deqing, then road | 2h45 to 3h30 |
| Shanghai Pudong | Transfer to Hongqiao, then rail and road | 4h00 to 4h45 |

![A group with overnight bags walking along a station platform seen from behind](/images/guide/groups-corporate-retreats-2.webp 'Sixty three minutes from Hongqiao is the number that makes a Moganshan offsite possible at all.')

Every figure is an estimate, ours rather than a published schedule, current as of 5 August 2026. They move with traffic and with the taxi queue at Deqing. Then there is your slowest colleague.

> Door-to-door times, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

The rail leg is the fast part: Hongqiao to Deqing in 63 to 80 minutes, 13 direct services a day, RMB 61 to 110 in second class. Then 30 to 40 minutes by road, because there is no through route. [Shanghai to Moganshan](/getting-here/from-shanghai/) has the timetables.

Pudong is the row that costs you: ninety minutes extra at each end, most of a working day across the trip.

## Why midweek is the whole argument

Occupancy across Moganshan guesthouses ran about 55 percent in 2024, against about 70 percent before 2021. The high-end segment managed 68 percent in 2025, the most recent number we hold. Nothing later is published.

> Sector occupancy, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

Set that against the week. The weekday and weekend split is severe, and holidays are the extreme case. High-end occupancy hit 94 percent over the May 2024 holiday, and National Day 2025 pushed daily footfall in Yucun 30 to 40 percent above normal.

If the year averages 55 percent and Saturdays run near full, midweek sits well below 55. That is arithmetic on published figures, not a measured weekday number. No one splits occupancy by day of the week.

So Tuesday to Thursday is where your booking power sits. If your dates are already fixed, skip to the checklist.

Rates follow. A mid-range room runs RMB 300 to 500 off season and passes RMB 1,000 at peak, high-end from RMB 1,500. Rooms are the only part of your per-head cost we can anchor. [Where to stay](/where-to-stay/) has the bands.

## Three things that break a group booking

Up to here it reads like an easy yes. None of these three appear on a booking page.

![A long communal table set for twelve inside a converted stone villa](/images/guide/groups-corporate-retreats-3.webp 'Houses here average about ten beds. A group of thirty is three separate bookings and three separate dinners.')

### The 08:00 to 18:00 window

No private vehicle enters the core scenic area. Everyone changes at one of three transfer centers, at Yucun, Fatou and Houwu, all running 08:00 to 18:00.

For one traveler that is an inconvenience. With thirty people on a coach, it becomes the schedule.

**If your rooms are inside the boundary, your group needs to clear central Shanghai by about 14:30.**

That is 18:00 minus 3h30, our own two numbers subtracted, not a rule anyone states. Check it against your date.

That cutoff kills the obvious plan. You cannot work Wednesday morning, leave at 17:00 and arrive for dinner. Go in the morning, or sleep the first night off the mountain.

The last mile from Deqing runs on the same clock.

| From Deqing station | Times | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Y1 shuttle | Seven departures a day, the last at 16:00. 30 to 40 minutes | RMB 10 |
| Taxi | About 30 minutes | About RMB 100 |

[Getting around Moganshan](/getting-here/getting-around/) has the detail.

A chartered coach is treated like any other private vehicle at the transfer centers, so build the change of vehicle into the arrival plan rather than hoping the charter gets waved through.

### Property size

This is the number to run first. Roughly 1,000 guesthouses share more than 10,000 beds. That is about ten each, and about 80 percent are owned by one household. Xiantan shows it: about 135 guesthouses among under 2,000 residents.

A 40-person group does not book a guesthouse here. It books three, or one of the few large properties.

| Property | Approximate keys | Where it is |
|---|---|---|
| naked Stables | 121 | On the mountain |
| naked Castle | 95 | On the mountain |
| Le Passage Mohkan Shan | 28 | On the mountain |
| Crowne Plaza Deqing Moganshan | 340 | Wukang town, about 20 km from the mountain |

> Property key counts and the Four Seasons announcement, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

Those are room counts rather than headcounts, and a full buyout is a question for each property rather than a standing offer.

Crowne Plaza, in that last row, has the capacity and sits in the county seat 20 km away. It solves your headcount and removes the reason you were coming. Four Seasons announced 90 keys here for 2030, no help now.

Splitting across two or three guesthouses is normal, and costs a vehicle move at every session start. Keep the houses in one village, because the drive between villages runs to half an hour at the far end.

### There is no evening economy

Chinese reporting on the villages is blunt about it: no night market, little food late. When dinner ends, the village is done.

> Absence of an evening economy in the villages, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

For a group, that has one consequence. Your property is the whole evening, every evening. Anything you have not booked will not appear. Warn anyone used to walking out after dinner in Shanghai.

Whether that is a drawback depends on what your two days are for. [Team building](/groups/team-building/) is the companion page.

## The meeting side is not published anywhere

No property on this mountain publishes what a planner needs: room sizes, AV, conference connectivity, catering or day delegate rates. That is not an oversight on their part. These are guesthouses, and a house averaging ten beds does not run a meetings sales desk.

So get all of it in writing before you commit, property by property, and treat a vague answer as a no. Ask for the dimensions of the room and a photograph of it empty.

## Planning checklist

Everything above, in the order you need it.

![A whiteboard and stacked chairs in a plain converted barn meeting space](/images/guide/groups-corporate-retreats-4.webp 'Meeting space exists but is rarely purpose built. Ask what the room actually is before you book it.')

| When | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing dates | Set Tuesday to Thursday | Weekends and holidays take the crowds and the rates |
| Choosing dates | Check the Golden Week calendar | May Day and National Day price out |
| Shortlisting | Ask which side of the scenic-area boundary the rooms are on | It decides whether the 18:00 cutoff applies |
| Shortlisting | Ask whether one property takes the full headcount | The average property has about ten beds |
| Six weeks out | Confirm every meal, including the last night | No food outside your property after dinner |
| Two weeks out | Fix the departure time from Shanghai | Arriving after 18:00 can mean sleeping off the mountain |
| Two weeks out | Bring Pudong arrivals in a day early | They travel on a different day from the rest |
| On the day | Brief the group on the vehicle change | Nobody with a suitcase expects to change vehicles |

## The verdict

Moganshan works as a two-night midweek offsite for a Shanghai team, not a one-night one. Travel eats too much of a single night, and then the 18:00 window takes the morning too.
