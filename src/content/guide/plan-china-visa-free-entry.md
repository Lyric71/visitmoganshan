# Visa-free entry to China, explained

*Last checked 5 August 2026. The 30-day scheme is written into law only to 31 December 2026.*

Two separate schemes now let people into mainland China without a visa. They cover different passports and run for different lengths, and one of them carries a condition the other does not. Both get called visa-free in English, and that alone accounts for most of the confusion. Mixing them up is the most common planning error we see.

The destination this site covers is Moganshan: a mountain in Deqing County, part of Huzhou prefecture, Zhejiang Province, about 60 km north of Hangzhou and 200 to 240 km from Shanghai. You cannot reach it without getting into China first, so we track the entry rules closely.

This page separates the two schemes and carries the full country list, dated tranche by tranche. Only one section below is about the mountain. (One warning on the name. Moganshan Road is an art district in Shanghai's Putuo District, about 200 km from the mountain, and it dominates the search results.)

One line before the detail. This is general information, not immigration advice. Rules change without notice. Only a Chinese embassy or consulate, or the National Immigration Administration, can tell you what applies to your passport on the day you travel.

## The two schemes, side by side

| Question | Unilateral visa-free | 240-hour visa-free transit |
|---|---|---|
| Who it covers | 50 countries as of February 2026 | 55 nationalities, including the United States |
| Running since | 1 December 2023, expanded in stages | 17 December 2024 |
| Length of stay | 30 days | 240 hours, which is 10 days |
| Onward ticket | Not required | Required, to a third country |
| Where you may travel | All of mainland China | 24 provinces and municipalities, entering at 65 ports |
| What you show at the border | A passport | A passport and the onward ticket |
| End date | Legislated to 31 December 2026 | No end date recorded in our sources |

## Which scheme applies to you

Work through it in this order and stop at the first yes.

![Several passports fanned out on a plain dark table](/images/guide/plan-china-visa-free-entry-2.webp 'Which of the two schemes you use is decided entirely by which passport you are holding.')

| Step | The question | If the answer is yes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is your country in the dated list below? | Enter for 30 days. No visa, no application, no fee. Stop here |
| 2 | If not: are you one of the 55 transit nationalities, and do you hold an onward ticket to a third country? | Enter for up to 240 hours, within the covered provinces |
| 3 | Neither | You need a visa, and you should start it before you book flights |

If you qualify under both, use the unilateral scheme. It gives you three times the stay, and it puts no condition on your ticket or on where you go.

## Scheme one: 30 days, no visa, 50 countries

China began a trial in late 2023. Six European and Asian countries were let in visa-free on 1 December 2023. Before that, only Singapore and Brunei held this kind of one-way exemption. One-way means China waives the visa without asking for the same in return.

The list grew from there in ten further rounds, the last on 17 February 2026. In November 2024 the permitted stay was doubled, from 15 days to 30. That doubling turned the scheme from a long weekend into a real trip, and the 30 days apply to every country in the table below.

| What it takes | Answer |
|---|---|
| Application | None |
| Fee | None |
| What you carry | A passport |
| Stay | 30 days, doubled from 15 in November 2024 |
| Countries covered | 50, listed by date in the next section |

You arrive with a passport and you are admitted.

> Stay length and the pre-2023 baseline, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

We publish the dates rather than a bare alphabetical list because the dates are what let you check us. A country list with no dates cannot be audited, and this one carries eleven separate start dates.

That table adds up to 50, and that is where the headline figure comes from. Other sites publish 52, and both counts are defensible.

| Count | What it includes |
|---|---|
| 50 | The eleven dated tranches in the table above |
| 52 | The same 50, plus Singapore and Brunei, whose exemptions predate the trial |

Either way the practical answer is the same. Find your country in the table, or you are on the other route.

A table on a travel site is not an immigration ruling. Put your own passport to a Chinese embassy or consulate before you book.

Two names are worth a second look. Canada and the United Kingdom were added on 17 February 2026. Plenty of published advice has not caught up, and still tells Britons and Canadians to apply for a visa. And the United States is not on the list at any date. Americans read "China is visa-free now" and assume it includes them. It does not.

## Scheme two: the 240-hour transit rule

If your country was not in that table, this is your route. On 17 December 2024 China replaced its old 72-hour and 144-hour transit rules with a single 240-hour scheme. Ten days, counted in hours, for 55 nationalities.

![A glowing but blank departures board above rows of empty seats in a night terminal](/images/guide/plan-china-visa-free-entry-3.webp 'Transit means transit. The rule is counted in hours, and it is checked before you board.')

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Effective from | 17 December 2024 |
| Replaced | The 72-hour and 144-hour transit schemes |
| Nationalities | 55, including the United States |
| Maximum stay | 240 hours |
| Entry points | 65 ports |
| Area covered | 24 provinces and municipalities |
| Condition | A confirmed onward ticket to a third country |

The United States is among the 55, which is the nationality everyone asks about. We do not hold the other 54 names, and guessing at them would be worse than leaving the gap open. Check the complete list with the National Immigration Administration before you rely on it.

### The onward-ticket condition

You must hold a confirmed ticket onward to a third country. Check this one first. A return flight home is the normal way to book a trip, and it does not work here.

| Routing | Qualifies |
|---|---|
| London to Shanghai to London | No. The last stop is where you started |
| Los Angeles to Shanghai to Seoul | Yes. Seoul is a third country |

The allowance is also written in hours, not in calendar days. Ten days sounds like ten dates on a calendar, and 240 hours is a clock that starts when you are admitted. The exact moment the count starts is not something this page can pin down, so plan against the shorter reading and leave a margin at the end.

### Where the 240 hours let you go

The area rule is what saves the scheme from being a long airport layover. You land at one of the 65 ports. From there you can move across all 24 covered provinces and municipalities, instead of being stuck in the city you flew into.

You are also not free to go everywhere. Twenty-four provinces leaves plenty of China outside the scheme, and a route that leaves the covered area needs a visa. Match your intended stops against the covered list first, then buy the internal transport.

## What the transit rule allows on a Shanghai trip

All of the above is abstract until you put it on a map. Shanghai is a municipality. Zhejiang is a province. Both are inside the 240-hour area, and Moganshan sits in Zhejiang.

| Stop | Where it sits | Covered |
|---|---|---|
| Shanghai | Shanghai municipality | Yes |
| Hangzhou | Zhejiang | Yes |
| Moganshan, in Deqing County | Zhejiang | Yes |
| Deqing station, your rail stop | Zhejiang | Yes |

Run a China visa free itinerary from Shanghai to Hangzhou to Moganshan and you cross exactly one provincial line. The scheme lets you cross it. It is the reason the mountain works on a transit stop at all.

The rail legs are short, and neither leaves the covered area.

| Leg | Time | Services a day |
|---|---|---|
| Shanghai Hongqiao to Deqing | 63 to 80 minutes | 13 direct |
| Hangzhou East to Deqing | 13 to 17 minutes | 16 |

[Getting here](/getting-here/) has the full timings, and our [five-day Shanghai, Hangzhou and Moganshan route](/itineraries/shanghai-hangzhou-moganshan/) puts them in order.

The arithmetic, if you are on the 240-hour clock, runs like this. Door to door from Shanghai Hongqiao to the mountain takes about 2 hours 45 to 3 hours 30 by rail, and the same again coming back. Two nights on the mountain is the sensible minimum, so budget three days of your ten for the Moganshan leg. That still leaves a week for the two cities. If you want the trip built around the transit rule specifically, the [visa-free China itinerary](/itineraries/visa-free-china/) page does that.

## The end date nobody has moved yet

The unilateral visa-free regime is currently legislated only to 31 December 2026. As of August 2026, no extension had been announced.

Our own read is that a lapse looks unlikely. The list has only ever grown, never shrunk, and the traffic backs it.

| Inbound traffic | Figure |
|---|---|
| Foreign-national entries to China, 2025 | About 41.0 million, against about 17.8 million in 2023 |
| Shanghai inbound visits, 2025 | 9.36 million, of which 7.14 million were foreign nationals |
| Zhejiang inbound overnight visitors, 2025 | 4.171 million, up 24.6 percent |

Unlikely is not a booking policy, though. Nothing has been legislated past 2026, so plan against the date that exists.

| If you travel | What to do |
|---|---|
| Before 31 December 2026 | The rules on this page apply as written |
| In 2027 or later | Re-check before you book anything you cannot refund |

> Scheme end date and 2025 inbound figures, BeyondBorder Group Ltd primary research, 1 to 5 August 2026.

## What visa-free entry does not solve

Getting in is the easy part now. Three things still catch visitors, and none of them depend on which scheme you used.

![An unattended hotel reception desk with a bell and a closed ledger](/images/guide/plan-china-visa-free-entry-4.webp 'Entry is the easy part now. Registration, payment and the guesthouse desk are where friction has moved.')

| What | Where it stands | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Paying for things | Foreign cards bind to Alipay and WeChat Pay, and have since 2023. Inbound tourists spent about RMB 80 billion that way in 2025 | Set it up at home, not in an airport queue |
| Accommodation registration | A joint directive of 24 May 2024 bars hotels from refusing foreign guests for lack of a license. Refusals are still reported | Get it confirmed in writing when you book |
| Tax refunds | The departure VAT refund minimum purchase fell from RMB 500 to RMB 200, and the cash ceiling doubled to RMB 20,000 | Only matters if you shop |

That directive came jointly from the Ministry of Public Security, the Ministry of Commerce and the National Immigration Administration. It is two years old. Refusals have not stopped. Moganshan runs on minsu (民宿), small owner-run guesthouses rather than hotel chains, so ask rather than assume the rule settles it. [Money and payments](/plan/money-and-payments/) covers the card setup.

Once the entry question is settled, [plan your trip](/plan/) takes over, from picking dates to what the week costs.
